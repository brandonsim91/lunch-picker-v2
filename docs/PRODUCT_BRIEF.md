# Lunch Picker V2 — Product Brief v0.1

## 1. Product statement

Lunch Picker V2 is a lightweight internal lunch decision tool for Samsung SDS colleagues working around Mapletree Business City.

It helps an individual or small group move from:

> "What should we eat?"

to:

> "Let's go here."

with minimal discussion.

---

## 2. Origin

The original Lunch Picker was created in 2025 after a recurring lunch problem was observed among colleagues.

The first prototype proved that even a very simple randomiser could reduce the friction of deciding where to eat.

V2 generalises that original use case beyond the initial group and redesigns the decision experience for wider SDS colleague use.

---

## 3. Problem

People working around MBC have many lunch options, but the abundance of choice creates repeated decision friction.

Common patterns include:

- asking the same "what should we eat?" question every day
- defaulting to the same few places
- rejecting suggestions one by one
- spending disproportionate time discussing a low-stakes decision
- forgetting places the group has not visited recently
- choosing somewhere that does not fit today's available time or budget

The problem is therefore not a lack of restaurant information.

It is **decision friction**.

---

## 4. Primary users

### Individual colleague

Wants a quick suggestion without thinking too much.

### Small lunch group

Needs a place acceptable to several people without a long discussion.

### Returning user

Already knows the area and wants help escaping repetitive choices.

---

## 5. Job to be done

> When I need lunch during the workday, help me quickly choose a suitable place nearby so I can stop debating and go eat.

---

## 6. Success criterion

A user should be able to move from opening Lunch Picker to a viable lunch decision in:

**under 30 seconds**

Initial qualitative indicators:

- the recommendation feels relevant to today's constraints
- users do not need to browse a long restaurant directory
- the group can accept, reject, or reroll quickly
- the result provides enough information to leave immediately

Possible later metrics:

- median time to decision
- recommendation acceptance rate
- rerolls per session
- most-used filters
- restaurant diversity over time

---

## 7. MVP user journey

### Step 1 — Open

No account required for the first MVP.

### Step 2 — State today's constraint

Examples:

- Quick
- Cheap
- Nearby
- Healthy
- Something new
- Anything

### Step 3 — Receive a shortlist

Return a small number of suitable options rather than a full directory.

### Step 4 — Decide

User can:

- choose an option
- reroll
- use **JUST PICK SOMETHING**

### Step 5 — Go

Show:

- restaurant name
- cuisine
- approximate walking time
- price band
- short reason for recommendation
- Google Maps link

---

## 8. Decision model

The MVP should primarily use deterministic filtering and weighting.

Example:

```text
Restaurant dataset
→ remove inactive / closed places
→ apply today's hard constraints
→ reduce recently visited options
→ apply soft preferences
→ rank / weight eligible candidates
→ return shortlist
```

For "JUST PICK SOMETHING":

```text
eligible candidate pool
→ weighted random selection
→ one final recommendation
```

An LLM is not required for this core decision.

---

## 9. Product principles

### Fast over comprehensive

Lunch Picker is not a restaurant discovery platform.

### Shortlists over directories

The product should reduce choices, not present more choices.

### Explain enough

A short reason such as "8 min walk · not visited recently · Japanese" helps users trust the recommendation.

### Maintainable data

Restaurant information should be structured and editable independently from the frontend.

### Appropriate complexity

Use deterministic software where deterministic software is sufficient.

### Preserve human agency

Users can always reroll, change constraints, or ignore the recommendation.

---

## 10. Out of scope for initial MVP

Do not add these until the core lunch decision loop works:

- AI chat assistant
- autonomous agents
- reservations
- payments
- delivery ordering
- complex authentication
- public restaurant reviews
- social networking
- recommendation explanations generated by an LLM
- live crowd prediction
- large-scale Singapore restaurant discovery

---

## 11. Initial geography

Primary area:

**Mapletree Business City and practical nearby lunch locations**

Potential nearby clusters may include:

- Pasir Panjang
- Alexandra
- ARC
- Alexandra Central
- Gillman Barracks

Exact inclusion should be based on realistic lunch-time travel constraints.

---

## 12. V1 → V2 hypothesis

V1 asked:

> "Which restaurant should I randomly choose?"

V2 asks:

> "Given today's constraints, which of the available lunch options is actually suitable?"

That distinction should guide the entire rebuild.
